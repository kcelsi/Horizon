The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine has been awarded to Mary E. Brunkow, Fred Ramsdell, and Shimon Sakaguchi for their pioneering discoveries in the field of peripheral immune tolerance. These discoveries are crucial for understanding how the immune system is regulated to prevent it from attacking the body's own organs, potentially leading to new treatments for autoimmune diseases. The laureates' research elucidated the key mechanisms that prevent the immune system from attacking the body, specifically focusing on peripheral immune tolerance, which operates outside the thymus and bone marrow.

The approach modifies hierarchical DSR models by employing feature-splitting and physical sparsity priors to correctly predict bifurcations and dynamics beyond training domains, even without explicit knowledge of control parameters. This work addresses a critical limitation in current time series models, enabling predictions in scenarios with drastic regime changes like crossing tipping points, which is crucial for scientific theories and applications in fields such as climate science and neuroscience. The paper identifies key failure modes in previous hierarchical DSR models that hinder control parameter learning and extrapolation. The proposed solution uses feature-splitting and physical sparsity priors, demonstrating success with various RNNs like PLRNNs and Neural ODEs.

arXiv has implemented a new policy penalizing authors with a one-year submission ban if their papers contain unverified AI-generated content, such as hallucinated citations or unreplaced placeholder data. Following the ban, future submissions will require prior acceptance by a peer-reviewed venue. This policy change by arXiv, a leading platform for academic preprints, directly addresses the growing challenge of maintaining research integrity with the rise of AI tools. It aims to ensure accountability for the content published in academic research. The policy specifically targets issues like hallucinated citations, residual AI comments, and placeholder text indicating unverified data. arXiv's code of conduct holds authors responsible for all content, regardless of its generation method.
