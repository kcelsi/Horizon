## [Huawei's 'Tao Law' Replaces Geometric Scaling with Time Scaling for Semiconductors](https://t.me/zaihuapd/42966) ⭐️ 9.0/10

At the 2026 International Circuits and Systems Symposium in Shanghai, Huawei introduced the 'Tao Law,' a new semiconductor development principle that substitutes 'geometric scaling' with 'time scaling.' The company has already designed and mass-produced 381 chips based on this principle over the past six years and plans to launch new Kirin mobile chips using 'logic folding' technology this autumn. This announcement is significant as it proposes a new paradigm to overcome the physical limitations of Moore's Law, potentially impacting the future trajectory of semiconductor innovation and performance. If successful, it could enable continued advancements in chip density and efficiency, affecting the entire tech industry, especially in areas like AI. The 'Tao Law' aims to achieve multi-level optimization across devices, circuits, chips, and systems by reducing the time constant, rather than solely relying on shrinking physical dimensions. Huawei projects that by 2031, chips based on this law will achieve transistor density equivalent to 1.4nm process nodes, utilizing 'logic folding' to physically fold and stack logic circuits.

**Background**: Moore's Law, an observation rather than a physical law, has historically predicted the doubling of transistors on integrated circuits approximately every two years, driving semiconductor miniaturization and performance gains. Geometric scaling refers to the practice of shrinking the physical dimensions of transistors and other components on a chip to increase density and speed. As components approach atomic scales, traditional geometric scaling faces fundamental physical limits.

## [China Proposes First Mandatory L3/L4 Autonomous Driving National Standard](https://t.me/zaihuapd/42972) ⭐️ 8.0/10

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology has completed the draft for the first mandatory national standard on intelligent connected vehicle autonomous driving systems, titled "Safety Requirements for Autonomous Driving Systems of Intelligent Connected Vehicles." The standard, which includes requirements for L3 human-machine handover and L4 autonomous risk management, is open for public comment until June 17 and is slated for implementation on July 1, 2027. This marks a significant regulatory shift in China's autonomous driving sector, moving from conceptual approvals to stringent safety mandates for L3 and L4 systems. The implementation of this standard will directly impact how autonomous vehicle manufacturers develop and deploy their technology, emphasizing safety and accountability. The new standard introduces a 'Safety Case' mechanism, requiring companies to systematically demonstrate the safety of their autonomous driving systems through a 'declaration-argument-evidence' approach. It specifically addresses the critical 'human-machine handover' for L3 and the autonomous risk management for L4 systems.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 4, 13:06

**Background**: Autonomous driving is categorized into different levels by the Society of Automotive Engineers (SAE), ranging from Level 0 (no automation) to Level 5 (full automation). Level 3 allows drivers to disengage under certain conditions but requires them to be ready to take over, while Level 4 operates autonomously within defined operational design domains without requiring human intervention. A Safety Case is a structured argument, supported by evidence, to justify that a system is acceptably safe for its intended use.
