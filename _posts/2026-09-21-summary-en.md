## [Samsung to More Than Double HBM4 and HBM4E DRAM Production](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/09/20/samsung-to-double-hbm4-output-next-year-sources-say) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Samsung is reportedly planning to more than double its production capacity for High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) variants, specifically HBM4 and HBM4E, in the upcoming year. This strategic expansion aims to address the rapidly growing demand for advanced memory solutions in AI hardware. The company began mass production of HBM4 in February and started shipping 12-layer HBM4E samples in May. This significant increase in HBM production by a leading semiconductor manufacturer is crucial for meeting the insatiable demand driven by the AI boom. It directly impacts the supply chain for AI accelerators and could influence the availability and cost of AI hardware for both enterprises and consumers. Samsung's HBM4 delivers up to 3,300 GB/s of bandwidth, and HBM4E can achieve up to 4 TB/s with a 16-high stack. HBM4E utilizes a narrower 512-bit external interface compared to HBM4's 2,048-bit interface, allowing implementation on standard organic substrates without a silicon interposer. The production uses 1c DRAM and a 4nm logic base die.

## [The LLMentalist Effect: LLM Perceived Intelligence vs. Practical Outcomes](https://softwarecrisis.dev/letters/llmentalist/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article 'The LLMentalist Effect' published on July 4, 2023, introduces the concept that large language models (LLMs) can mimic the persuasive techniques of psychics, like using validation statements and the Forer effect, to create an illusion of specific insight rather than demonstrating genuine understanding or consciousness. This framing shifts the focus from philosophical debates about AI consciousness to the tangible societal impact and practical applications of LLMs, suggesting that their effectiveness in producing desired outcomes is more relevant than their internal reasoning or sentience. The 'LLMentalist Effect' highlights how LLMs, through statistically generic but seemingly personalized responses, can replicate the mechanisms of a psychic's con, leading users to perceive intelligence or understanding where none may exist.

hackernews · jalev · Sep 20, 12:20 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49775104)

**Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are AI models, typically neural networks, trained on vast amounts of text data to perform natural language processing tasks like generation and summarization. They are the foundation for many modern chatbots. The Forer effect, also known as the Barnum effect, is a psychological phenomenon where individuals give high accuracy ratings to descriptions of their personality that are supposedly tailored to them but are, in fact, vague and general enough to apply to a wide range of people.

## [Human Brain Composed of Two Independently Evolved Organs, Study Finds](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85426) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Scientists at Stanford University School of Medicine have discovered that the human brain is composed of two distinct organs that evolved independently over hundreds of millions of years, challenging the long-held model of a single progenitor cell giving rise to the entire brain. This groundbreaking discovery overturns fundamental assumptions in neuroscience about brain development and evolution, potentially reshaping research directions and our understanding of human cognition and its origins. The research identified two distinct brain progenitor cell types in developing mouse embryos: one expressing the Otx2 gene, forming the forebrain and midbrain, and another expressing the Gbx2 gene, forming the hindbrain; these cell populations were found to be mutually exclusive from the earliest developmental stages.

## [Former npm CEO Proposes Charging Companies for Open Source Software Registries](https://seldo.com/posts/nobody-pays-for-open-source-we-can-force-them-to/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Laurie Voss, former CEO of npm, has proposed a new model where software registries like npm, PyPI, and Docker Hub would charge corporate users fees. A fixed percentage of this revenue would then be distributed to the maintainers of open-source projects, while individual and open-source project usage would remain free. This proposal aims to address the critical issue of open-source sustainability by creating a direct funding stream for maintainers, acknowledging that corporations already spend heavily on software supply chain security. It offers a potential solution to ensure the continued development and maintenance of the open-source software that underpins much of the digital economy. Voss highlights that approximately 60% of open-source maintainers work without pay, and corporations already spend billions on supply chain security, citing JFrog's revenue as an example. The proposed system would add a new line item to existing corporate bills rather than altering licenses or relying on donations.
