The Diátaxis framework provides a structured and effective approach to writing technical documentation, organizing content into four distinct types: tutorials, how-tos, reference, and explanation. This framework is significant because it brings clarity and practicality to technical writing, helping teams create high-quality documentation that is easier for users to navigate and for contributors to maintain. Diátaxis categorizes documentation based on user needs, distinguishing between learning (tutorials, explanations) and doing (how-tos, reference) tasks, and the framework's author is actively working on translations into other languages.

NetBSD has officially released version 11.0, introducing significant updates including enhancements to its npf(7) firewall with layer 2 and user/group filtering, a new MICROVM kernel for x86 systems capable of booting in approximately 10 milliseconds, and official support for the RISC-V architecture. This major release signifies continued development and relevance for the NetBSD operating system within the systems research and software engineering communities, particularly with its advancements in security features and expansion to new hardware architectures like RISC-V. The npf(7) firewall improvements offer more granular control, while the MICROVM kernel's rapid boot time could enable new use cases for lightweight virtualization on x86 platforms.

A new paper reveals that current evaluation benchmarks for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in radiology report generation are flawed, as they can assign high scores to generic reports that omit clinically meaningful terms and may introduce bias. This discovery is significant because it undermines the reliability of current VLM evaluation metrics in a critical medical domain, potentially leading to the deployment of AI systems that fail to accurately convey essential clinical information or even introduce harmful biases. The paper highlights that existing metrics reward repetitive, 'normal' reports and fail to detect the erasure of clinically significant but rare terms, leading to reports of no clinical utility. A framework is proposed to measure term erasure and biased term introduction.

**Background**: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are AI systems that can process and understand both visual and textual information. In medical imaging, VLMs are being developed for tasks like radiology report generation (RRG), aiming to automate the creation of descriptive reports from images like X-rays. However, AI models, including VLMs, are known to sometimes 'hallucinate' or generate incorrect information, and can also exhibit biases present in their training data.
