## [ORL accelerates diffusion LLM inference to 2,788 tokens/s on RTX 5090](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1x7qgr0/research_2788_tokenss_on_a_single_rtx_5090_with_a/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have introduced On-Policy Residual Learning (ORL), an open-source method that significantly boosts diffusion language model (dLLM) inference speeds, achieving up to 2,788 tokens/s on a single RTX 5090 GPU and 7,708 tokens/s on 8x B200 GPUs. This breakthrough is accompanied by a custom inference engine, dlmrt, and open-sourced code, models, and evaluation pipelines. ORL's dramatic speed improvements on consumer and enterprise hardware democratize access to high-performance dLLMs, potentially enabling new real-time applications and reducing the cost of deploying advanced AI models. This advancement could significantly impact the field of natural language processing by making powerful generative models more accessible and efficient. ORL utilizes on-policy residual learning by refining previous predictions as residual context and employs Adjacent Entropy Contraction to accelerate convergence, enabling more tokens per forward pass. The dlmrt inference engine optimizes for single-request decoding by capturing the denoising loop in a CUDA graph for GPU execution, achieving 1.2–1.6x speedup over SGLang/vLLM in comparisons.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/arty_photography · Oct 16, 20:03

**Background**: Diffusion language models (dLLMs) iteratively denoise corrupted text to generate coherent sequences, offering an alternative to traditional autoregressive LLMs that generate tokens one by one. On-Policy Residual Learning (ORL) is a technique adapted from reinforcement learning where a model learns corrections (residuals) to its existing policy based on its own current outputs, ensuring the learning process stays aligned with the inference behavior.

## [AI Claude Uncovers NIST ECDSA Curve Seed Origins](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The AI model Claude has determined that the seeds for several NIST ECDSA curves, including P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384, and P-521, were generated using a simple Unix timestamp to seed the C library's `lrand48` random number generator. This discovery resolves a long-standing mystery surrounding the creation of these widely used cryptographic parameters. This revelation debunks numerous conspiracy theories that speculated about the deliberate inclusion of backdoors or specific mathematical properties in the NIST ECDSA curves. It provides a clear, albeit simple, explanation for their origin, impacting the trust and understanding of widely deployed cryptographic standards. The seeds were generated by seeding `lrand48` with a Unix timestamp, and the process was completed in under half an hour, suggesting a straightforward, non-malicious generation method. However, the seed for the B-163 curve remains unexplained, with one theory suggesting a typo during publication.

hackernews · ahlCVA · Oct 16, 18:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122800)

**Background**: ECDSA (Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm) is a cryptographic algorithm used for digital signatures, providing security for digital communications. NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology) has standardized several elliptic curves for use in cryptography, including the P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384, and P-521 curves. A Unix timestamp is a way of representing time as the number of seconds that have elapsed since the Unix epoch (January 1, 1970).
