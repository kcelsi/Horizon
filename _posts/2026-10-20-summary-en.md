It represents a new approach to AI performance optimization that could be adopted across the industry. Maxwell functions similarly to a Just-In-Time (JIT) compiler but uses a model to determine optimizations rather than fixed heuristics, operating by modifying program binaries during execution. Formal verification is a method used to mathematically prove the correctness of hardware or software systems against a formal specification.

## [Inception Launches Mercury 3: Diffusion LLM Exceeds 1k TPS with 1M Token Context](https://www.inceptionlabs.ai/blog/introducing-mercury-3) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Inception has released Mercury 3, a large diffusion language model that achieves a generation speed exceeding 1000 tokens per second and boasts a 1 million token context window. This new model, approximately 40 times larger than Mercury 1, has been made available via the Inception API and platforms like OpenRouter. Mercury 3's impressive performance metrics, particularly its high tokens per second generation speed and extensive context window, position it as a significant advancement in the LLM space. This could lead to more efficient and capable AI applications, potentially impacting various industries that rely on natural language processing. The model offers competitive input pricing at $0.2 per million tokens and has demonstrated benchmark performance exceeding models like GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5. Diffusion language models adapt diffusion processes for text generation, contrasting with traditional sequential token prediction.

**Background**: Diffusion language models (DLMs) are a type of generative model that adapt diffusion processes, originally popularized for image generation, to natural language processing. Unlike autoregressive models that predict tokens sequentially, DLMs typically involve a noise-to-text transformation process. The context window of an LLM refers to the maximum number of tokens it can consider at one time, influencing its ability to process long inputs.
