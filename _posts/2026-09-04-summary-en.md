## [Developer Ports 1993 Amiga Game to Godot with LLM Assistance](https://babyloniantwins.com/blog/porting-a-1993-amiga-game-to-godot/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A developer has successfully ported their 1993 Amiga game, originally written in MC68000 assembly, to the Godot engine in a single evening, with further refinement over subsequent weekends. The process involved using Claude Fable 5, an LLM, to assist with code analysis and even generate the first draft of the project's documentation. This project highlights the growing capability of LLMs in understanding and working with legacy code, potentially aiding in game preservation and the modernization of older software. It demonstrates a novel approach to retro game development by bridging decades-old technology with modern game engines and AI tools. The LLM was able to assemble the original 68000 assembly code using `vasm` and achieve a byte-identical binary to the original game's executable, with a minor 108-byte discrepancy attributed to the original game's runtime snapshot saving method. The developer also released the original game for free.

**Background**: The Amiga was a popular line of personal computers known for its advanced graphics and sound capabilities, widely used for gaming and multimedia in the late 1980s and early 1990s. MC68000 assembly language is a low-level programming language used to directly control the Motorola 68000 microprocessor, common in many 1980s and 1990s computers and consoles. The Godot Engine is a free and open-source, cross-platform game engine used for creating 2D and 3D games.

## [Grounding LLMs with JEPA-based World Models in Simulation](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w69gvd/grounding_llms_with_jepabased_world_models/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A proposal suggests training Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) within physics simulations to create world models that encode physical principles, which are then used to condition Large Language Models (LLMs). This approach aims to imbue LLMs with a grounded understanding of physics beyond statistical token correlations. This approach could lead to LLMs with a more robust and intuitive grasp of the physical world, potentially accelerating downstream learning and enabling more reliable AI systems in domains requiring physical reasoning. It addresses the fundamental limitation of LLMs lacking true understanding despite their linguistic fluency. The proposed method involves training a JEPA model to predict future state representations in an abstract embedding space within a physics simulator like MuJoCo, rather than predicting raw pixels or tokens. The learned representations, encoding concepts like object permanence and momentum, would then serve as a conditioning signal for an LLM.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Full_Promotion4522 · Sep 3, 14:45

**Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at processing and generating text but often lack a true understanding of the physical world, akin to the 'Mary's Room' thought experiment where knowledge is propositional but not experiential. Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) are a self-supervised learning approach that learns by predicting abstract representations of data, aiming to capture high-level semantic structures rather than just surface-level details. Physics engines like MuJoCo are used to simulate physical interactions for research in areas such as robotics and machine learning.

South Korea's Minister of Trade, Industry and Energy, Ahn Duk-geun, announced a national semiconductor cluster plan to build a second production base in the southwestern region. This initiative involves attracting 800 trillion KRW (approximately 3.52 trillion RMB) in corporate investment to construct four memory wafer fabrication plants. This massive investment signals South Korea's ambition to maintain its leading position in the global semiconductor market, particularly in DRAM, by capitalizing on projected market growth. The expansion could significantly impact global supply chains and competition in the memory chip sector. The plan aims to double DRAM production within five years, anticipating a more than fourfold increase in the global memory market. The government will also contribute 30 trillion KRW (approximately 132.12 billion RMB) over the next 15 years to support the initiative.
