## [Mathematicians Prove Finite-Time Blow-Up for Unforced Navier-Stokes Equations](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge have published a proof demonstrating finite-time blow-up for the unforced Navier-Stokes equations, a significant development in fluid dynamics research. This breakthrough addresses a key aspect of the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, potentially impacting our understanding of fluid behavior and turbulence. The proof specifically addresses the 'unforced' version of the equations, differentiating it from previous work that may have involved external forces to induce blow-up.

hackernews · trotro · Nov 20, 15:34 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50576232)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations describe the motion of viscous fluids and are fundamental to fluid dynamics. The existence and smoothness problem, a Millennium Prize Problem, questions whether these equations always have smooth, bounded solutions or if they can break down with unbounded solutions in three-dimensional space. Finite-time blow-up refers to a scenario where a solution to the equations becomes infinitely large in a finite amount of time.

A new paper demonstrates that language models can autonomously rewrite their own weights to improve performance without traditional training methods like gradient descent. The model iteratively tests edits on practice problems, submitting only successful modifications to achieve performance gains. This research challenges the conventional understanding of model training and opens up new possibilities for self-improving AI systems. It could lead to more efficient and autonomous AI development, but also raises significant AI safety concerns regarding unintended modifications. The paper claims the model rewrites its weights based on its own reasoning, without gradients, and improves accuracy over eight consecutive sessions. One commenter noted the improvement was specifically to reduce reasoning trace length to avoid token limits, drawing parallels to prior work.

## [KVM Vulnerability on AMD Hosts Allows VM to Read Host Memory](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/new-linux-kvm-flaw-cve-2026-99312-lets.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A critical KVM vulnerability, CVE-2026-99312, has been disclosed, enabling virtual machines on AMD hosts with nested virtualization enabled to read host memory. A fix has been merged into newer kernel versions, including stable kernel 7.3.5, released on November 18th. This vulnerability poses a significant security risk by allowing guest VMs to access sensitive host memory, potentially leading to data breaches. Users on affected AMD systems should apply patches or disable nested virtualization until updates are available. The vulnerability specifically affects AMD hosts with nested virtualization enabled and was demonstrated by researchers reading approximately 127 GB of host memory from within a VM. While upstream fixes are available, distributions like Debian and Ubuntu have not yet integrated them.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 20, 14:23

**Background**: KVM (Kernel-based Virtual Machine) is a virtualization infrastructure in Linux that allows the kernel to function as a hypervisor. Nested virtualization enables running a hypervisor (like KVM or Hyper-V) inside a virtual machine, which is useful for testing or running multiple layers of virtualization.
